::rubrica Dunsinane. Ante-room in the castle.

::rubrica Enter a Doctor of Physic and a Waiting-Gentlewoman.

::fala Doctor
I have two nights watched with you, but can perceive
no truth in your report. When was it she last walked?

::fala Gentlewoman
Since his majesty went into the field, I have seen
her rise from her bed, throw her nightgown upon her, unlock
her closet, take forth paper, fold it, write upon't, read it,
afterwards seal it, and again return to bed; yet all this
while in a most fast sleep.

::fala Doctor
A great perturbation in nature, to receive at once
the benefit of sleep and do the effects of watching! In this
slumbery agitation, besides her walking and other actual
performances, what, at any time, have you heard her say?

::fala Gentlewoman
That, sir, which I will not report after her.

::fala Doctor
You may to me, and 'tis most meet you should.

::fala Gentlewoman
Neither to you nor any one, having no witness
to confirm my speech.

::rubrica Enter Lady Macbeth, with a taper.

                      Lo you, here she comes!
This is her very guise, and, upon my life, fast asleep. Observe
her; stand close.

::fala Doctor
How came she by that light?

::fala Gentlewoman
Why, it stood by her: she has light by her continually;
'tis her command.

::fala Doctor
You see, her eyes are open.

::fala Gentlewoman
Ay, but their sense is shut.

::fala Doctor
What is it she does now? Look, how she rubs
her hands.

::fala Gentlewoman
It is an accustomed action with her, to seem thus
washing her hands: I have known her continue in this a
quarter of an hour.

::fala Lady Macbeth
Yet here's a spot.

::fala Doctor
Hark! she speaks: I will set down what comes
from her, to satisfy my remembrance the more strongly.

::fala Lady Macbeth
Out, damned spot! out, I say! One: two:
why, then 'tis time to do't. Hell is murky. Fie, my lord,
fie! a soldier, and afeard? What need we fear who knows
it, when none can call our power to account? Yet who
would have thought the old man to have had so much
blood in him?

::fala Doctor
Do you mark that?

::fala Lady Macbeth
The thane of Fife had a wife; where is she
now? What, will these hands ne'er be clean? No more
o' that, my lord, no more o' that: you mar all with this
starting.

::fala Doctor
Go to, go to; you have known what you should
not.

::fala Gentlewoman
She has spoke what she should not, I am sure of
that: heaven knows what she has known.

::fala Lady Macbeth
Here's the smell of the blood still: all the
perfumes of Arabia will not sweeten this little hand. Oh,
oh, oh!

::fala Doctor
What a sigh is there! The heart is sorely charged.

::fala Gentlewoman
I would not have such a heart in my bosom for
the dignity of the whole body.

::fala Doctor
Well, well, well,—

::fala Gentlewoman
Pray God it be, sir.

::fala Doctor
This disease is beyond my practice: yet I have
known those which have walked in their sleep who have
died holily in their beds.

::fala Lady Macbeth
Wash your hands; put on your nightgown;
look not so pale: I tell you yet again, Banquo's buried;
he cannot come out on 's grave.

::fala Doctor
Even so?

::fala Lady Macbeth
To bed, to bed; there's knocking at the gate:
come, come, come, come, give me your hand: what's done
cannot be undone: to bed, to bed, to bed. [Exit.]

::fala Doctor
Will she go now to bed?

::fala Gentlewoman
Directly.

::fala Doctor
Foul whisperings are abroad: unnatural deeds
Do breed unnatural troubles: infected minds
To their deaf pillows will discharge their secrets:
More needs she the divine than the physician.
God, God forgive us all! Look after her;
Remove from her the means of all annoyance,
And still keep eyes upon her. So good night:
My mind she has mated and amazed my sight:
I think, but dare not speak.

::fala Gentlewoman
                             Good night, good doctor.

::rubrica Exeunt.
