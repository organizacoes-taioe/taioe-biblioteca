::rubrica Juliet's chamber.

::rubrica Enter Nurse.

::fala Nurse
Mistress! what, mistress! Juliet! fast, I warrant her, she:
Why, lamb! why, lady! fie, you slug-a-bed!
Why, love, I say! madam! sweet-heart! why, bride!
What, not a word? you take your pennyworths now;
Sleep for a week; for the next night, I warrant,
The County Paris hath set up his rest
That you shall rest but little. God forgive me,
Marry, and amen, how sound is she asleep!
I needs must wake her. Madam, madam, madam!
Ay, let the county take you in your bed;
He'll fright you up, i' faith. Will it not be?

::rubrica Undraws the curtains.

What, dress'd! and in your clothes! and down again!
I must needs wake you. Lady! lady! lady!
Alas, alas! Help, help! my lady's dead!
O, well-a-day, that ever I was born!
Some aqua-vitæ, ho! My lord! my lady!

::rubrica Enter Lady Capulet.

::fala Lady Capulet
What noise is here?

::fala Nurse
                    O lamentable day!

::fala Lady Capulet
What is the matter?

::fala Nurse
                    Look, look! O heavy day!

::fala Lady Capulet
O me, O me! My child, my only life,
Revive, look up, or I will die with thee.
Help, help! call help.

::rubrica Enter Capulet.

::fala Capulet
For shame, bring Juliet forth; her lord is come.

::fala Nurse
She's dead, deceased, she's dead; alack the day!

::fala Lady Capulet
Alack the day, she's dead, she's dead, she's dead!

::fala Capulet
Ha! let me see her. Out, alas! she's cold;
Her blood is settled and her joints are stiff;
Life and these lips have long been separated.
Death lies on her like an untimely frost
Upon the sweetest flower of all the field.

::fala Nurse
O lamentable day!

::fala Lady Capulet
                  O woeful time!

::fala Capulet
Death, that hath ta'en her hence to make me wail,
Ties up my tongue and will not let me speak.

::rubrica Enter Friar Laurence and Paris, with Musicians.

::fala Friar Laurence
Come, is the bride ready to go to church?

::fala Capulet
Ready to go, but never to return.
O son, the night before thy wedding-day
Hath death lain with thy wife: see, there she lies,
Flower as she was, deflowered by him.
Death is my son-in-law, death is my heir;
My daughter he hath wedded: I will die,
And leave him all; life, living, all is Death's.

::fala Paris
Have I thought long to see this morning's face,
And doth it give me such a sight as this?

::fala Lady Capulet
Accurst, unhappy, wretched, hateful day!
Most miserable hour that e'er time saw
In lasting labour of his pilgrimage!
But one, poor one, one poor and loving child,
But one thing to rejoice and solace in,
And cruel death hath catch'd it from my sight!

::fala Nurse
O woe! O woeful, woeful, woeful day!
Most lamentable day, most woeful day,
That ever, ever, I did yet behold!
O day! O day! O day! O hateful day!
Never was seen so black a day as this:
O woeful day, O woeful day!

::fala Paris
Beguiled, divorced, wronged, spited, slain!
Most detestable death, by thee beguiled,
By cruel cruel thee quite overthrown!
O love! O life! not life, but love in death!

::fala Capulet
Despised, distressed, hated, martyr'd, kill'd!
Uncomfortable time, why camest thou now
To murder, murder our solemnity?
O child! O child! my soul, and not my child!
Dead art thou! Alack, my child is dead;
And with my child my joys are buried!

::fala Friar Laurence
Peace, ho, for shame! confusion's cure lives not
In these confusions. Heaven and yourself
Had part in this fair maid; now heaven hath all,
And all the better is it for the maid:
Your part in her you could not keep from death;
But heaven keeps his part in eternal life.
The most you sought was her promotion,
For 'twas your heaven she should be advanced:
And weep ye now, seeing she is advanced
Above the clouds, as high as heaven itself?
O, in this love, you love your child so ill,
That you run mad, seeing that she is well:
She's not well married that lives married long,
But she's best married that dies married young.
Dry up your tears, and stick your rosemary
On this fair corse, and, as the custom is,
In all her best array bear her to church:
For though fond nature bids us all lament,
Yet nature's tears are reason's merriment.

::fala Capulet
All things that we ordained festival,
Turn from their office to black funeral:
Our instruments to melancholy bells;
Our wedding cheer to a sad burial feast;
Our solemn hymns to sullen dirges change;
Our bridal flowers serve for a buried corse,
And all things change them to the contrary.

::fala Friar Laurence
Sir, go you in; and, madam, go with him;
And go, Sir Paris; every one prepare
To follow this fair corse unto her grave:
The heavens do lour upon you for some ill;
Move them no more by crossing their high will.

::rubrica Exeunt Capulet, Lady Capulet, Paris, and Friar.

::fala First Musician
Faith, we may put up our pipes, and be gone.

::fala Nurse
Honest good fellows, ah, put up, put up;
For, well you know, this is a pitiful case. [Exit.]

::fala First Musician
Ay, by my troth, the case may be amended.

::rubrica Enter Peter.

::fala Peter
Musicians, O, musicians, 'Heart's ease, Heart's
ease:' O, an you will have me live, play 'Heart's ease.'

::fala First Musician
Why 'Heart's ease'?

::fala Peter
O, musicians, because my heart itself plays 'My
heart is full of woe:' O, play me some merry dump, to
comfort me.

::fala First Musician
Not a dump we; 'tis no time to play now.

::fala Peter
You will not then?

::fala First Musician
No.

::fala Peter
I will then give it you soundly.

::fala First Musician
What will you give us?

::fala Peter
No money, on my faith, but the gleek; I will
give you the minstrel.

::fala First Musician
Then will I give you the serving-creature.

::fala Peter
Then will I lay the serving-creature's dagger on
your pate. I will carry no crotchets: I'll re you, I'll fa
you; do you note me?

::fala First Musician
An you re us and fa us, you note us.

::fala Second Musician
Pray you, put up your dagger, and put out
your wit.

::fala Peter
Then have at you with my wit! I will dry-beat
you with an iron wit, and put up my iron dagger. Answer
me like men:
'When griping grief the heart doth wound
And doleful dumps the mind oppress,
Then music with her silver sound'—
why 'silver sound'? why 'music with her silver sound'?—
What say you, Simon Catling?

::fala First Musician
Marry, sir, because silver hath a sweet sound.

::fala Peter
Pretty! What say you, Hugh Rebeck?

::fala Second Musician
I say, 'silver sound,' because musicians
sound for silver.

::fala Peter
Pretty too! What say you, James Soundpost?

::fala Third Musician
Faith, I know not what to say.

::fala Peter
O, I cry you mercy; you are the singer: I will
say for you. It is 'music with her silver sound,' because
musicians have no gold for sounding:
'Then music with her silver sound
With speedy help doth lend redress.' [Exit.]

::fala First Musician
What a pestilent knave is this same!

::fala Second Musician
Hang him, Jack! Come, we'll in here; tarry
for the mourners, and stay dinner. [Exeunt.
